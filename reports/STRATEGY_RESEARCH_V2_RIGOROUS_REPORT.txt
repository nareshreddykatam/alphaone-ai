======================================================================
METHODOLOGY
======================================================================

This is a STRICTER methodology than the original multi-strategy pass
(scripts/research_multi_strategy.py / reports/MULTI_STRATEGY_RESEARCH_RESULTS.txt),
specifically designed to close a real leakage risk in that original
pass: walk-forward folds alone do not prevent a researcher from
eyeballing many folds and unconsciously favoring a strategy/parameter
choice that happens to look good across them.

1. Chronological, non-shuffled 60/20/20 split into TRAIN / VALIDATION /
   OUT-OF-SAMPLE TEST. Never shuffled (shuffling a time series before
   splitting is itself a leakage bug -- see tests/leakage/test_split_no_shuffle.py
   for the existing regression test this project already carries).

2. For every strategy with a genuinely free parameter, a small grid
   (2-3 values per parameter, at most 2 parameters -- never an enormous
   brute-force search) is backtested on VALIDATION only (trained
   understanding: TRAIN is available for a strategy that needs fitting,
   but every candidate here is rule-based with no fitting step, so the
   grid is evaluated directly on VALIDATION with a minimum-15-trade
   eligibility floor to avoid selecting a lucky tiny sample). The
   winning parameters are FROZEN.

3. The frozen strategy is evaluated EXACTLY ONCE against the untouched
   OOS region. This number is never used to go back and retune.

4. Walk-forward folds (4, forward-chaining) are cut ONLY from the OOS
   region -- so fold-level stats never reuse TRAIN/VAL bars.

5. Long/short trades are broken out separately from every OOS run.

6. Regime segmentation labels each OOS bar bull/bear (200-period EMA
   slope over 20 bars) and high/low volatility (rolling ATR/price
   percentile vs. its own 200-bar median), then attributes each closed
   trade to the regime active at its entry time.

7. Parameter sensitivity re-runs OOS at each frozen parameter's
   immediate grid neighbors.

8. A trade-order bootstrap (500x reshuffle of the OOS trade PnL
   sequence) estimates how much of the observed max drawdown is a
   function of trade ORDER vs. a structural property of the trades
   themselves -- a robustness diagnostic, not proof of anything.

9. Cross-strategy correlation of OOS daily-return series flags
   near-duplicate strategies before final selection.

Cost model (unchanged, reused from services/backtester/engine.py's
ExchangeSpec default -- the SAME model every other backtest in this
project uses): taker fee 0.04%, slippage 0.01%, average funding 0.01%
per funding interval. Execution timing: next-bar-open fill (see
docs/execution_semantics.md) -- no look-ahead.

IS the softer risk this v2 pass exists to close: a human choosing
default parameters while able to see how they perform across the whole
dataset. v2's TRAIN/VALIDATION/OOS discipline removes that risk for
every candidate re-examined here.

======================================================================
HONEST LIMITATIONS
======================================================================

- The OOS window is a single, contiguous ~7-month period (2026-01 to
  2026-08 for both timeframes). It is genuinely out-of-sample relative
  to TRAIN/VAL, but it is still ONE historical period -- a different
  7-month slice of BTC's history could show different results for every
  strategy here, including S06. This report does not, and cannot, prove
  future performance.
- Parameter grids were deliberately small (2-3 values per parameter,
  1-2 parameters) per the explicit instruction against "enormous
  brute-force searches." A different, more exhaustive search might find
  different (better OR worse) parameter regions -- not attempted here by
  design.
- The regime segmentation (200-EMA-slope bull/bear, ATR-percentile
  high/low-vol) is one reasonable but not the only valid way to define
  "market regime." A different regime definition could attribute trades
  differently.
- The trade-order bootstrap tests sensitivity to ORDER only, using the
  REAL trades' PnL values -- it does not simulate different market paths
  or different signals, only different orderings of the same outcomes.
  It is a narrow robustness check, not a general Monte Carlo simulation
  of the strategy's future.
- Backtests here are single-strategy, single-position (services/
  backtester/engine.py's existing design) -- they do not model what
  happens when MULTIPLE independent strategies (e.g. S05 and S06 both
  live) hold correlated positions simultaneously in the real paper-
  trading system. Portfolio-level interaction across strategies was not
  evaluated in this task.
- S09's headline OOS numbers (PF 1.23, positive walk-forward folds) look
  competitive with S06's but rest on only 23 OOS trades, with one fold's
  PF driven by just 4 trades -- explicitly flagged above as not
  credible evidence on its own, kept RESEARCH_ONLY rather than promoted.
- This report does not, and cannot, promise any future profitability,
  maximum profitability, 100% accuracy, or risk-free operation for any
  strategy, including the two marked PRODUCTION_ELIGIBLE. "PRODUCTION_ELIGIBLE"
  here means "cleared this project's documented, evidence-based bar for
  generating live signals" -- never a guarantee.
