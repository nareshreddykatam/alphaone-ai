2. CRITICAL FIXES
------------------------------------------------------------
(1) Gap-through-stop -- verified with the spec's exact examples:
    LONG  SL=98,  next open=94  -> exit ~94  (was 98)
    SHORT SL=102, next open=106 -> exit ~106 (was 102)
    Normal (non-gapped) stop touches still fill at the stop level, unchanged.

(2) Real historical funding -- hand-calculated and verified both directions:
    $10,000 notional, rate=0.01% -> $1.00 funding cost.
    LONG + positive rate  -> pays   (cost +$1.00)
    LONG + negative rate  -> receives (cost -$1.00)
    SHORT + negative rate -> pays   (cost +$1.00)
    SHORT + positive rate -> receives (cost -$1.00)
    Point-in-time correct: entry-exactly-at-funding-timestamp is not
    charged; positions closed before a funding event are not charged;
    positions spanning multiple events are charged for each; a funding
    rate dated after the dataset's end is never used.

14. KNOWN LIMITATIONS
------------------------------------------------------------
CRITICAL, discovered this phase, NOT fixed (flagged for follow-up):
  The risk-engine kill switch never auto-resets once tripped. Confirmed
  every baseline's continuous 3-year backtest actually stopped trading in
  Aug-Oct 2023 (within the first 5-8 weeks) and stayed locked out for the
  remaining ~2.9 years. This makes the section-8 continuous-run numbers
  NOT representative of 3-year behavior -- use section 9 (walk-forward,
  immune to this bug) as the credible multi-period evidence instead. Not
  fixed here because it wasn't one of the two issues this phase was
  scoped to fix, and the right reset policy (daily reset vs. hard stop vs.
  configurable) deserves deliberate design, not a reactive patch.

Carried over from Phase 2 (see docs/known_limitations.md for full list):
  OI capped at ~30 days, no historical liquidations, no TP2/TP3/partial
  exits, exchange fee/slippage values are labeled assumptions, take-profit
  doesn't get the same gap-aware fill logic as stop-loss, Garman-Klass
  volatility can produce NaN on some bars, no scaler in the ML pipeline
  (none needed for tree models).

15. INTERPRETATION
------------------------------------------------------------
- Sample sizes are generally small (1-25 trades per continuous run per
  timeframe/strategy; 24 folds of a handful of trades each in walk-forward).
  None of this rises to statistical significance in a formal sense -- no
  significance test was run, and none should be claimed.
- Where a mean walk-forward return is positive (EMA and Donchian+ADX, both
  timeframes), it is small (+0.06% to +0.50% per ~3-5 week fold) relative
  to the spread across folds (up to +5.22%/-2.74%), meaning results are
  NOT stable fold-to-fold -- a handful of large winning folds visibly pull
  the mean up while the MAJORITY of individual folds for trend-following
  are still negative (9/24 and 15/24 profitable respectively -- i.e. even
  the "best" result has more losing folds than winning ones at 1h).
- Transaction costs are material: fees alone ran $100-$550 against
  $10,000 accounts across these runs -- a meaningful share of any modest
  edge, consistent with these being high-frequency-ish mechanical rules
  trading noise more than a real signal.
- RSI is the most consistently negative across every cut (continuous runs,
  both walk-forward timeframes, all leaning loss-making).
- Buy-and-hold's continuous-run number (+135%) reflects BTC's price
  appreciation over this specific 3-year window, not strategy skill, and
  should not be read as "buy-and-hold beats the baselines" in any
  risk-adjusted sense -- it carries the full drawdown of the underlying
  asset with no risk management at all.
- The kill-switch bug (section 14) means the continuous-run trade counts
  and "stability across timeframes" cannot be assessed from section 8
  alone -- section 9's walk-forward folds are the only results in this
  report immune to that confound, and even those show no strategy with a
  convincingly stable, cost-surviving edge.

16. RECOMMENDATION FOR PHASE 3
------------------------------------------------------------
1. Is the backtester trustworthy? Mostly yes, with one important caveat.
   Execution timing, fees, slippage, gap-through-stop, and funding all now
   reconcile exactly against hand-calculated scenarios, and 13 automated
   sanity checks + the full leakage suite pass. The one real trust gap is
   the kill-switch lockout (section 14): CONTINUOUS multi-year backtests
   are not currently trustworthy for strategies that can trip a
   daily-loss/drawdown limit early. WALK-FORWARD backtests are trustworthy
   today because each fold resets risk state.
2. Are the baseline results stable? No. Walk-forward shows roughly
   coin-flip fold-level outcomes for every strategy, with wide dispersion
   (multi-percent swings both directions per fold) and no strategy
   clearing 50%+ profitable folds convincingly across both tested
   timeframes.
3. Are any baselines showing a potentially meaningful edge? Donchian+ADX
   trend-following is the closest thing to a positive signal (positive
   mean walk-forward return on both 1h and 4h, and 15/24 profitable folds
   at 4h) but this is NOT verified as a real edge -- it is one strategy,
   two timeframes, driven partly by a few large winning folds, with no
   significance test applied. It should be treated as "worth a closer,
   dedicated look" -- not as a finding.
4. What weaknesses remain? The kill-switch lockout (critical, flagged as
   a follow-up task); no TP2/TP3/partial exits; take-profit gap handling
   asymmetric with stop-loss; OI/liquidation history limited by the
   exchange, not the code; small trade counts throughout that limit any
   statistical claim.
5. Is the foundation ready for ML research? For LEAKAGE and EXECUTION
   correctness: yes -- the data pipeline, feature engine, and backtester
   have survived adversarial and reconciliation testing at a level that
   should be trustworthy input for a model. For BACKTESTING a trained
   model over long historical windows: not yet -- the kill-switch issue
   should be resolved first, or Phase 3 must exclusively evaluate models
   via the walk-forward path (which is already unaffected) rather than
   any continuous multi-year run.

FINAL RULE, ANSWERED HONESTLY: No baseline demonstrates a verified,
cost-surviving statistical edge. Buy-and-hold's large continuous-run
number is the underlying asset's price move, not a strategy result.
Donchian+ADX trend-following is the one strategy worth a closer, dedicated
look in Phase 3 -- but it is reported here as "worth investigating,"
not as "found an edge."
